The assessment

Standard: AS91504 — Analyse issues in safety management for outdoor activity to devise safety management strategies

Credits: 3  |  Level: 3 Achievement Standard

Through an outdoor activity trip, you'll identify the risks involved, analyse the causal factors behind them, and devise strategies to minimise or eliminate them at different levels of responsibility — from personal choices right up to national-level management.

Types of risk

Perceived risk: an individual's own sense of how much risk exists within an activity.
Absolute risk: the risk that exists before you begin to manage it or make it safer.
Actual (real) risk: the risk that remains once you've applied risk management strategies to make the activity safer.

Task 1 — Identify the risks

Identify and write down all the risks you can see being involved in your trip.

Task 2 — Complete the risk assessment form

For each risk you identify: explain why it's a risk, then work out management strategies at each level — personal/self management, school management, activity-provider management (e.g. a dive/tour operator), and government/national-level management.

Research your location

Research your trip location and look for cultural impacts, environmental impacts, and physical impacts — add article links and key information as you go.
