Year 9 Health & Physical Education

Our driving question this year: how can we become the most healthy versions of ourselves? Across four 10-week units we'll look at what influences our participation and movement, and build strategies to improve hauora for ourselves and others — through hands-on activities, reflection, and real experience rather than sitting still in a classroom.

Our four units this year

Hauora 10 weeks

Driving question: What does it mean to be healthy?

We'll build our understanding of Te Whare Tapa Whā — Taha Wairua, Taha Tinana, Taha Hinengaro, and Taha Whānau — and use it to make sense of our own hauora. You'll take part in a range of activities and build a portfolio reflecting on how each one affected your wellbeing.

What you'll do:

  • Define well-being, hauora, and healthy in your own words, with a drawing and example for each
  • Fill in the empty Te Whare Tapa Whā model — define each of the four dimensions, give at least five examples for each, and colour it in
  • Identify examples of what impacts your own hauora across all four dimensions
  • After each activity, reflect using the sentence starters on how it impacted each dimension (Taha Wairua, Taha Tinana, Taha Hinengaro, Taha Whānau), signed off by your Learning Leader as an exit card

How you're assessed, in short:

  • Emerging: comes prepared and engaged, can identify what impacts their own hauora using Te Whare Tapa Whā
  • Effective: can also describe using examples how their own hauora is impacted
  • Exemplary: engaged exceptionally well, and can evaluate by setting goals and future expectations

Health Promotion 10 weeks

Driving question: How can I best take action to improve my Taha Tinana?

This unit is about taking action. You'll set goals and take part in activities designed to improve your own Taha Tinana (physical wellbeing), while building your understanding of health and hygiene practices, how your body responds to physical activity, and why regular activity matters for hauora.

What you'll do:

  • Define Taha Tinana, hygiene, and healthy, with a drawing and example for each
  • Brainstorm everything that makes up a healthy person, around an outline avatar
  • Map hygiene practices (body odour, teeth, physical activity, feet, coughing, period, face & skin, washing hands) — why each matters for your Taha Tinana and how it shows manaakitanga to others
  • Describe the body's physical responses to activity — heart rate, temperature, breathing rate, blood pressure, hormonal — why they happen and their benefits
  • Take part in physical challenges to record your pre- and post-results, then set goals and an action plan to improve

How you're assessed, in short:

  • Emerging: comes prepared and engaged, can identify actions to improve their own Taha Tinana by setting goals
  • Effective: can also describe using examples how their Taha Tinana is impacted by implementing changes
  • Exemplary: engaged exceptionally well, and can evaluate by making future changes and expectations

Socio-Ecological Perspective 10 weeks

Driving question: How do barriers and enablers to physical activity in New Zealand have an impact at a personal, interpersonal, and societal level?

We'll get curious about what stops people being active and what helps them get active — and look at the effect this has on individuals, groups, and wider society. You'll create opportunities and challenges for others as part of building this understanding.

What you'll do:

  • Define personal, interpersonal, and society, with a drawing and example for each
  • Fill in the socio-ecological model with examples at the personal, interpersonal, and societal level
  • Define barriers, enablers, equal, and equitable, with a drawing and example for each
  • Design a piece of visual art showing the difference between equal and equitable
  • Read a real news article on the cost of sport in NZ (Mary's story) and identify who's affected at a personal vs. interpersonal level, the biggest barrier, and its impact at personal, interpersonal, and societal levels
  • After each activity, reflect on the barriers and enablers that influenced your ability to participate

How you're assessed, in short:

  • Emerging: comes prepared and engaged, can identify what and who makes up each perspective
  • Effective: can also describe using examples the barriers and enablers to physical activity in NZ
  • Exemplary: engaged exceptionally well, and can describe the impacts of these barriers and enablers at a personal, interpersonal, and societal level

Attitudes & Values 10 weeks

Driving question: In what ways do the decisions we make affect ourselves and others, and how can we consciously make choices that promote positive outcomes for ourselves and others?

Using Hellison's model of social responsibility, we'll build our understanding of what it means to respect ourselves, other people, and our environment — and reflect on our own level of social responsibility through a portfolio of experiences.

What you'll do:

  • Define social, responsibility, personal, attitudes & values, and society, with a drawing and example for each
  • Fill in the values circle — list the top 5 values held by yourself, your whānau, your friends, and society
  • Work through Hellison's Levels of Social Responsibility (Level 0: Irresponsible, through to Level 5: Outside of PE) and identify what you'd hear and see at each level
  • After each activity, use the reflection loop: which level you worked at, the value that influenced you, the impact on others, and your goal for next lesson

How you're assessed, in short:

  • Emerging: comes prepared and engaged, can identify how their level of social responsibility impacts others
  • Effective: can also describe using examples how their level of social responsibility impacts others
  • Exemplary: engaged exceptionally well, and can evaluate by setting goals and future expectations
