Year 9 Health & Physical Education

Our driving question this year: how can we become the most healthy versions of ourselves? Across four units we'll look at what influences our participation and movement, and build strategies to improve hauora for ourselves and others — through hands-on activities, reflection, and real experience rather than sitting still in a classroom.

Our four units this year

Hauora 10 weeks

Driving question: What does it mean to be healthy?

We'll build our understanding of Te Whare Tapa Whā — Taha Wairua, Taha Tinana, Taha Hinengaro, and Taha Whānau — and use it to make sense of our own hauora. You'll take part in a range of activities and build a portfolio reflecting on how each one affected your wellbeing.

At the end of each lesson, you'll reflect using sentence starters and get this signed off by your learning leader as an exit card.

Health Promotion 10 weeks

This unit is about taking action. You'll set goals and take part in activities designed to improve your own Taha Tinana (physical wellbeing), while building your understanding of health and hygiene practices, how your body responds to physical activity, and why regular activity matters for hauora.

Expect a mix of practical sessions and reflection, working toward your own personal activity goal.

Socio-Ecological Perspective 10 weeks

Driving question: How do barriers and enablers to physical activity in New Zealand have an impact at a personal, interpersonal, and societal level?

We'll get curious about what stops people being active and what helps them get active — and look at the effect this has on individuals, groups, and wider society. You'll create opportunities and challenges for others as part of building this understanding.

Attitudes & Values 10 weeks

Driving question: In what ways do the decisions we make affect ourselves and others, and how can we consciously make choices that promote positive outcomes for ourselves and others?

Using Hellison's model of social responsibility, we'll build our understanding of what it means to respect ourselves, other people, and our environment — and reflect on our own level of social responsibility through a portfolio of experiences.

What you'll need: Alfriston College PE uniform, a pen, and a chromebook. A phone is not an adequate learning device.
