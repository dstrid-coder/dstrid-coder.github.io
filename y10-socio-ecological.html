Learning objectives: build knowledge of what the socio-ecological perspective is and how it's an influencing factor; identify what and who makes up each perspective; get curious about barriers and enablers to physical activity in NZ and their impacts at a personal, interpersonal, and societal level.

Success criteria: you've engaged positively with others and completed both the practical and theory learning.

Structure & guide

  • Task 3 — Barriers, enablers, equal, equitable
  • Task 4 — Equal vs. equitable, in art
  • Task 5 — A real-world case study
  • Reflection — after every activity
Task 1 — Define the vocabulary

For personal, interpersonal, and society: write a definition in your own words, draw an image, and give an example.

Task 2 — Fill in the socio-ecological model

Identify as many examples as you can for each of the three layers — personal, interpersonal, and societal.

Task 3 — Barriers, enablers, equal, equitable

Write a definition, draw an image, and give an example for each of these four words.

Task 4 — Equal vs. equitable, in art

Design and create a piece of visual art that demonstrates the difference between equal (everyone gets the same thing) and equitable (everyone gets what they need to succeed).

Task 5 — A real-world case study

Read the news article about the cost of sport in NZ. Identify who's discussed at a personal vs. interpersonal level, the biggest barrier to participation, and how that barrier impacts at a personal and interpersonal level — then discuss as a class how it impacts at a societal level.

Reflection — after every activity

Reflect on the barriers and enablers that influenced your ability to participate in that activity.

Resources

  • Personal: your own attitudes, knowledge, physical skills, and mindset
  • Interpersonal: the direct influence of whānau, friends, peers, and teachers
  • Societal: wider community structures, NZ laws, media, culture, and facilities
