Learning objectives: build knowledge and make sense of what hauora is and how it's impacted, using Te Whare Tapa Whā to reflect on your own hauora.

Success criteria: you've engaged positively with others and completed both the practical and theory learning.

Structure & guide

  • Task 1 — Define the vocabulary
  • Task 2 — Fill in Te Whare Tapa Whā
  • Task 3 — What impacts your hauora
  • Reflection — after every activity
Task 1 — Define the vocabulary

For well-being, hauora, and healthy: write a definition in your own words, draw an image, and give an example.

Task 2 — Fill in Te Whare Tapa Whā

Define each of the four dimensions (Taha Wairua, Taha Tinana, Taha Hinengaro, Taha Whānau), give at least five examples for each, and colour in your model.

Task 3 — What impacts your hauora

Identify as many examples as you can of what impacts your own hauora, sorted under each of the four dimensions.

Reflection — after every activity

Use the sentence starters to reflect on whether the activity had a positive or negative impact on your hauora, how it impacted each dimension specifically, and what you'd need to do next lesson for a better outcome. Get this signed off by your Learning Leader as an exit card.

Resources

  • Te Whare Tapa Whā was developed by Sir Mason Durie — our wellbeing is compared to a wharenui, with four strong walls needed to keep it standing
  • Taha Tinana: physical (how your body grows, feels, and moves)
  • Taha Hinengaro: mental & emotional (how you think, feel, and communicate)
  • Taha Whānau: family & social (who you belong with and care for)
  • Taha Wairua: spiritual (your values, beliefs, and sense of purpose)
