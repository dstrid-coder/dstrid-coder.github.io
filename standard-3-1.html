3.1 — Analyse a NZ Health Issue

AS91461 asks you to analyse a health issue that matters in Aotearoa — why it's a public concern, what's driving it, how it affects wellbeing, and what could actually change it. This page is a scaffold to help you structure your own thinking. It won't write your answer for you — the ideas, evidence, and analysis need to be yours.

Part 1 — Nature of the health issue

Introducing the issue

The New Zealand health issue I am analysing is ___________.
This issue is significant in New Zealand because ___________.
It particularly affects ___________ (specific population).
In New Zealand, statistics show that ___________ (source, year).
Compared to other groups, ___________ are disproportionately affected because ___________.
Internationally, this issue is similar to / differs from New Zealand in that ___________.

Explaining why it's a public concern

This issue is considered a matter of public concern because ___________.
It impacts not only individuals but also society through ___________.
The increasing / decreasing trend of ___________ highlights that ___________.

Challenging taken-for-granted assumptions

Identifying assumptions

A common assumption about this issue is that ___________.
Many people believe that ___________.
This assumption suggests that individuals are responsible for ___________.

Challenging assumptions (Excellence-level thinking)

However, this assumption ignores the influence of ___________.
This belief oversimplifies the issue because ___________.
Evidence from ___________ (source) suggests that ___________.
This demonstrates that the issue is influenced more by structural factors than individual choice.
Challenging this assumption reflects the value of social justice because ___________.

Part 2 — Determinants of health

You need to cover at least two determinants.

Defining the determinant

One major determinant of health contributing to this issue is the ___________ determinant.
The ___________ determinant refers to ___________.
In the context of this issue, it relates to ___________.

Explaining how it contributes

This determinant contributes to the issue by ___________.
For the specified population, this means that ___________.
As a result, individuals experience ___________.

Explaining why it contributes (depth)

This occurs because ___________.
The underlying reason for this is ___________.
Power structures / policies / social norms influence this by ___________.
This shows how the socio-ecological perspective applies, as individuals are influenced by ___________ levels of society.
Therefore, the issue is not simply about personal choice, but about ___________.

Linking to evidence

According to ___________ (year), ___________.
This evidence highlights that ___________.
This statistic demonstrates the unequal impact on ___________.

Repeat this same structure for your second determinant.

Part 3 — Implications of the issue

Impacts on wellbeing (hauora)

Taha tinana (physical): Physically, this issue impacts individuals by ___________. In the short term, ___________. In the long term, ___________.

Taha hinengaro (mental & emotional): Mentally and emotionally, individuals may experience ___________. This can lead to ___________. Over time, this may result in ___________.

Taha whānau (social relationships): This issue affects relationships by ___________. Families/whānau may experience ___________. This can strain relationships because ___________.

Taha wairua (spiritual, if relevant): Spiritually, individuals may feel ___________. This may disconnect them from ___________.

Wider societal implications

At a societal level, this issue impacts New Zealand by ___________.
Economically, it costs ___________.
The healthcare system is affected because ___________.
Certain groups are advantaged because ___________, while ___________ are disadvantaged because ___________.
This demonstrates inequity as ___________.

Part 4 — Health-promoting strategies

Introducing a strategy

One health-promoting strategy that could address this issue is ___________.
This strategy would involve ___________.
The aim of this strategy is to ___________.

Linking the strategy to determinants

This strategy directly addresses the ___________ determinant by ___________.
It reduces the impact of ___________ because ___________.
By targeting structural factors rather than individuals, it promotes equity through ___________.

Reflecting health promotion practice

This strategy reflects effective health promotion because it ___________.
It aligns with the Ottawa Charter action area of ___________.
It enables / mediates / advocates by ___________.

Explaining how it creates more equitable outcomes

This would bring about more equitable outcomes because ___________.
It supports social justice by ___________.
Over time, this would reduce disparities by ___________.
Therefore, this strategy moves beyond awareness and instead ___________.

Linking back to underlying health concepts

Use these throughout your whole piece, not just at the end:

This reflects the socio-ecological perspective because ___________.
This demonstrates how determinants of health interact to ___________.
Through the lens of hauora, it is clear that ___________.
This highlights the importance of collective responsibility rather than individual blame.
Ultimately, this issue shows that health is shaped by ___________.

Excellence-level linking sentences

When considering the broader social context, it becomes evident that ___________.
The interaction between ___________ and ___________ deepens the impact of this issue.
This reveals a systemic inequity within New Zealand society.
Therefore, meaningful change must occur at a structural level rather than solely focusing on behaviour change.
This analysis demonstrates that improving outcomes requires both policy reform and cultural change.

This scaffold gives you sentence structures to build from — it's not a template to fill in word-for-word, and it definitely won't write your analysis for you.
