NCEA & Endorsements

A plain-language guide for students and parents to how NCEA works, and specifically how endorsements work in Health and Physical Education at Alfriston College.

The basics Course endorsement Certificate endorsement Health & PE specifics University Entrance

The basics

NCEA (the National Certificate of Educational Achievement) is New Zealand's main secondary school qualification, made up of three levels — Level 1, 2, and 3 — usually completed across Years 11, 12, and 13.

Within each subject, students are assessed against a number of standards. Each standard is worth a number of credits, and is graded as one of:

  • Not Achieved — no credits awarded
  • Achieved — full credits awarded
  • Merit — full credits, at a higher standard of performance
  • Excellence — full credits, at the highest standard of performance

Standards are either internally assessed (marked by teachers at school, usually through practical work, projects, or booklets, throughout the year) or externally assessed (marked by NZQA, usually through an end-of-year exam).

Course (subject) endorsement

Course endorsement recognises strong performance in one specific subject, in one school year. To gain it, a student generally needs:

  • 14 or more credits at Merit or Excellence (for a Merit endorsement), or 14 or more credits at Excellence (for an Excellence endorsement)
  • at least 3 of those credits from an externally assessed standard, and at least 3 from an internally assessed standard
  • all within a single school year

Course endorsement shows next to that subject on the student's Record of Achievement — it doesn't require gaining the full NCEA certificate itself.

Certificate endorsement

Certificate endorsement recognises strong performance across an entire NCEA level, not just one subject. To gain it, a student needs 50 or more credits (at the level of the certificate, or higher):

  • Merit endorsement: 50+ credits at Merit or Excellence
  • Excellence endorsement: 50+ credits at Excellence

This shows directly on the NCEA certificate itself, alongside the overall qualification.

Health & PE specifics

There's one exception to the course endorsement rule above that matters specifically for Physical Education: Level 2 and Level 3 PE courses made up entirely of internally assessed standards are exempt from the internal/external split. That means a PE student can still gain course endorsement from 14+ credits at Merit or Excellence, even without any external (exam) credits — which matters here, since our Level 3 PE course (3.1, 3.3, 3.4, 3.7, 3.8) is entirely internally assessed.

Health doesn't have this exemption, so Health students need at least 3 credits from an external standard as well as 3 from internal standards to gain course endorsement. This is usually covered naturally, since our Level 3 Health course includes an externally assessed exam standard alongside the internally assessed 3.1, 3.3, and 3.4.

University Entrance

To gain University Entrance (UE), a student needs:

  • NCEA Level 3, with 14 or more credits in each of three approved subjects (both Health and PE are approved subjects)
  • 10 literacy credits at Level 2 or above (5 reading, 5 writing)
  • 10 numeracy credits at Level 1 or above
This page is a general guide, not the full official rules. For the complete, current criteria — and for any changes to NCEA in upcoming years — the definitive source is nzqa.govt.nz. If anything on this page conflicts with what NZQA says, follow NZQA.
