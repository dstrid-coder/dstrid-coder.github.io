The assessment

Standard: AS91464 v2 — Analyse a contemporary ethical issue in relation to well-being

Credits: 4  |  Level: 3 Achievement Standard

You'll write a magazine feature for a general-interest magazine that analyses your chosen ethical issue in relation to well-being. You'll be assessed on:

  • the differing perspectives on your issue
  • the implications of current practice on the well-being of those directly affected, others associated with them, and wider society

Your feature needs to identify at least two groups who support and two groups who oppose your issue, explaining the ethical foundations (attitudes, values, beliefs) behind each. You'll support your analysis with evidence from research published in the last 5 years, referenced properly.

Achieved / Merit / Excellence, in short:

  • Achieved: explain the differing perspectives, and explain the implications for those affected, others, and society.
  • Merit: provide a balanced view of perspectives with some reference to underlying health concepts.
  • Excellence: examine perspectives with insight into their ethical foundations, and link your examination to the underlying health concepts throughout.

Structure & guide

Introduce your ethical issue

What is the issue? Why would people consider it a matter of public concern? What is the current legal position in New Zealand? How does it compare internationally? Why does it raise ethical concerns?

Two groups in support of the issue

For each group: who holds this perspective? What do they believe? Why do they believe it? What evidence supports their view? (For Excellence: what attitudes, values, and beliefs sit underneath their position?)

Two groups against the issue

Same structure as above, from the opposing side.

Implications

Work through implications for the individual, interpersonal (friends and whānau), and society — each time asking: who is affected, how does it link to hauora, what are the short-term and long-term effects, and what's positive versus negative?

Conclusion

Summarise your magazine feature.

Worked example — Euthanasia

This shows the kind of research depth you're aiming for — naming real organisations, what they believe, and why.

Support: Voluntary Euthanasia Society NZ (VESNZ)

Believe: in the right to choose assisted dying via medical professionals.

Why: to end suffering at the end of life and maintain dignity.

Support: Yes for Compassion

Believe: terminally ill New Zealanders should have choice and control at the end of life, including requesting life-ending medication in the final six months.

Why: often linked to personal experience and values.

Against: DEFEND NZ

Believe: the End of Life Choice Act has serious implications for families, the health system, and the elderly and disabled.

Why: concerned that easier access to euthanasia is especially risky in a context of ageism and social isolation of older people.

Against: The Nathaniel Centre

Believe: euthanasia will be practised unevenly across social groups, and risks outweigh benefits.

Why: advocates for better health care rather than assisted dying.

Ethical scenarios warm-up

A few discussion prompts to get you thinking about what makes something an ethical issue in the first place: public hospitals not performing elective cosmetic surgery; government funding directed to roads rather than parks and reserves; tax cuts for multi-home owners alongside cuts to school lunch funding; requiring full-faced photos for driver's licences or passports; non-vaccinated teachers losing their jobs. For each: is it an ethical issue, and why or why not?

A reminder: any facts, statistics, or organisation details on this page should be double-checked against the original source before you use them in your own assessment — websites and positions can change, and AI-sourced summaries (including this page) can get details wrong.
