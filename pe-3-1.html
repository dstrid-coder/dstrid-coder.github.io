The assessment

Standard: AS91498 v2 — Evaluate physical activity experiences to devise strategies for lifelong well-being

Credits: 4  |  Level: 3 Achievement Standard

This assessment has two parts. In Part A, you'll critically evaluate your physical activity experiences to date — childhood play, sport, fitness, recreation, outdoor pursuits, aquatics, dance, kapa haka, and cultural festival performances all count. You'll examine who or what influenced you towards each activity, question and challenge assumptions (gender, height/build, stereotypes, expectations), and make judgements about the impact on your well-being, including how the dimensions of hauora interrelate.

In Part B, you'll devise strategies for lifelong well-being — connecting clearly back to Part A, considering what's next for you (study, work, travel), and exploring the barriers and enablers to staying physically active into the future.

Achieved / Merit / Excellence, in short:

  • A

    examine your experiences, consider influences and reasons for participation, and make judgements about their impact on lifelong well-being, supported with specific examples.

  • M

    comprehensively examine your experiences and make coherent, clearly connected judgements about their impact.

  • E

    include clearly identified assumptions that are questioned and challenged, and make coherent and insightful judgements that build on that questioning.

Structure & guide

  • Part A — Brainstorm and timeline your experiences
  • Part A — Tell the story, question the assumptions
  • Part A — Link to hauora
  • Part B — Devise your strategies
Part A — Brainstorm and timeline your experiences

Brainstorm every physical activity you've taken part in across your life so far, then plot them on a timeline: 0–3, 3–6, 6–10, 10–14, and 14–18/now. This gives you the raw material for the rest of Part A.

Part A — Tell the story, question the assumptions

For each key experience, tell it as a story — who (parents, coaches, friends) or what (goals, media) played a role in your uptake, and what impact it had on your well-being. Where you question an assumption, be clear from the start of the paragraph what the assumption is, how you're advantaged or disadvantaged by it, and how you're challenging it — don't just tack it on at the end.

Part A — Link to hauora

Show how your experiences connect across the dimensions of hauora — taha tinana, taha hinengaro, taha whānau, and taha wairua — and how those dimensions interrelate.

Part B — Devise your strategies

Link clearly back to Part A. Consider what's next for you (university, work, travel) and what physical activity needs you'll have. Unpack the barriers (things that get in the way) and enablers (things that help) you'll face, and build a clear, structured plan to keep physical activity part of your life. The Ministry of Health website is a good source to draw on here.

Resources

Practical activity ideas by age group — used in class to explore what's age-appropriate:

  • 0–3 years: parachute games with animals, throw and catch, hop-skip-jump, general play items
  • 5–8 years: duck duck goose, throw and catch, balloon relays, raging river, bench pull, big mat roll, noodle hockey, moon ball / moon ball soccer
