The assessment

Standard: AS91463 v2 — Evaluate health practices currently used in New Zealand

Credits: 5  |  Level: 3 Achievement Standard

You'll write a report that evaluates three different health practices currently used in New Zealand to treat or prevent a health circumstance you choose — things like pain management, an addiction, a mental illness, a physical illness, or reproductive health.

Your three practices must be:

  • One Western Scientific Medicine (WSM) practice — e.g. drug treatment, surgery, physiotherapy
  • One Complementary/Alternative Medicine (CAM) practice — e.g. chiropractic, acupuncture, naturopathy
  • One Traditional Medicine (TM) practice — e.g. rongoā Māori, traditional Chinese medicine

For each practice, your report needs to cover:

  • the philosophy or knowledge foundation behind it
  • the procedures involved in treating/preventing your health circumstance
  • financial cost, availability, and access
  • advantages and disadvantages, and how effective it's likely to be
  • impact on wellbeing — short and long-term, including relationships and impact on society
  • societal attitudes, acceptability, and any controversy around it

Achieved / Merit / Excellence, in short:

  • Achieved: explain the philosophy, procedures, cost/access, advantages/disadvantages, and attitudes for each practice, with some supporting evidence.
  • Merit: compare advantages and disadvantages in depth, draw supported conclusions, and use credible, current evidence throughout.
  • Excellence: connect your evaluation to hauora, socio-ecological perspective, health promotion, and attitudes & values, and draw well-justified conclusions.

Structure & guide

Task 1 — Choose your health circumstance and practices

Pick a health circumstance, then choose one WSM, one CAM, and one TM practice used to treat or prevent it in New Zealand.

Task 2 — Philosophy and procedures

For each practice: what's the underlying philosophy or knowledge foundation? What actually happens during treatment — what are the procedures involved?

Task 3 — Cost, effectiveness, and wellbeing impact

What does it cost, and how accessible is it? Weigh up the advantages and disadvantages, and how effective the practice is likely to be. What's the impact on wellbeing — for the individual (short and long-term), their relationships, and wider society?

Task 4 — Societal attitudes

How accepted is this practice in NZ society? Do people have a right to choose it? Is there any controversy or challenge around it?

Task 5 — Conclusion

Draw justified conclusions about your three practices and their impact on hauora, socio-ecological perspective, health promotion, and attitudes and values. Which practice(s) come out as most effective, and why?

Resources

Worked example — Pain Management

Pain management is a strong health circumstance to research because there are real options across all three categories. Here's research already gathered on five candidate practices — pick three (one from each category) to build your own report around, or use this as a model for researching your own health circumstance.

Physiotherapy WSM

Uses manual therapy, exercise, and techniques like electrical stimulation to help restore movement and reduce pain. Roughly $30–60 per session.

Medication (e.g. ibuprofen) WSM

Reduces inflammation and blocks pain signals, making movement easier. Free for children 13 and under at many clinics; otherwise available at any pharmacy.

Chiropractic CAM

Hands-on and non-invasive therapy focused on the spine and joints to reduce tension, improve mobility, and relieve pain. Consults roughly $60–150, follow-ups around $40–85.

Acupuncture / cupping CAM

Acupuncture uses thin needles at specific points on the body; cupping uses suction cups (often on the back) to boost blood flow and release muscle tension. Roughly $70–100 for acupuncture, $20–100 for cupping depending on session length.

Rongoā Māori TM

A traditional, holistic Māori healing system using rākau (trees), rongoā (medicinal plants), massage, karakia, and guided discussion to treat both physical and underlying wellbeing. Roughly $30–60 per session, sometimes ACC-covered.
